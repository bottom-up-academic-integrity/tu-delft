The Normalization of Genocide and the Necessity of Disruption In the face of injustice, it is disruptive and civil disobedient actions like the recent campus encampment that are necessary to force structural change. November 2026

On October 5th, students of Delft Student Intifada (DSI) established an encampment at the TU Delft Aula. This was not an arbitrary act. It was a response to a profound moral failure: the Executive Board’s (CvB) September 30th statement1 , which reaffirmed a policy allowing most ongoing collaborations with Israeli partner institutions to continue until their scheduled completion. This policy, the result of a 15-month delay, makes our university materially and financially complicit in an ongoing genocide. As staff in contact with the CvB have stated, the new policy amounts to a “downplaying and trivialization of Israel’s oppression, apartheid and genocide.”

The encampment is a necessary act of civil disobedience. Its power lies not just in its physical presence, but in its symbolism, the names of the killed, and the the olive trees planted in their memory. These are not merely political statements, they are necessary attempts to break the ongoing spell of normalization. The genocide relies on a process of desensitization that tries to make us "get on with our lives" while it is being carried out in our name. The encampment forces us to stop, to feel, and to refuse. It is because of such protests that we remain sensitive, that we do not allow the unbearable to become part of the norm.

The CvB’s response has been telling. They have sent multiple letters to the university community, framing the peaceful and orderly protest as a violation of campus rules and a threat to safety. This letters to us read as another attempt to weaponize feelings of discomfort, emphasizing the safety of the Jewish community in a way that creates a false and deeply racist dichotomy:2 This rhetoric implicitly posits that the Jewish community is represented by a settler-colonial state, and that a protest against genocide is inherently antisemitic. It is a supremacist logic that elevates our comfort above the lived reality of Palestinians facing extermination. At a certain point, one must ask: what does it mean to be comfortable while our institution is materially implicated in genocide?

The CvB’s language might sound civilized, neutral, and reasonable to many of us; they didn’t evict the encampment after all. This civility nonetheless masks a simple reality: TU Delft remains materially tied to entities engaged in the slaughter of Palestinians. In March 2026, University of Gent completed its withdrawal from a European research consortium involving an Israeli partner following a negative human rights assessment.3 On October 1st 2026, Utrecht University formally withdrew from a research consortium involving the Israeli Ministry of Health.4 These decisions demonstrate that ending such collaborations is possible. It simply requires the will to do so. TU Delft has chosen a policy of delay, inaction, and a refusal to name the genocide5 it is aiding with academic and research collaborations.

We, the staff of TU Delft for Integrity, stand with the students protesting the university’s complicity in genocide. We support acts of disobedience that disrupt business as usual at TU Delft, challenging the normalization of genocide in Palestine and demanding that the university confront its own complicit. We wholeheartedly cheer on the student-led encampment and the spirit of restless refusal it embodies. In the face of genocide, civil disobedience is one of the few ways we can bring about genuine and timely change. To those who are annoyed or feel unsafe, we must ask you to reflect on the source of that discomfort. The encampment is not the danger; the genocide being carried out with our university’s complicity is.

We celebrate the encampment for what it is: the diligent, caring work of students who built the Palestinian house, organized events, and maintained a peaceful presence through cold nights to stand against their institution’s moral failure. This is what collective action looks like. It keeps us alert and connected. In the face of injustice, it is disruptive and civil disobedient actions like this that are necessary to force structural change. We thank the students for their integrity and humanity.
