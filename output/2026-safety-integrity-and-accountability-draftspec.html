 Concept for insitutional polling system. Our thoughts on how an insitutional polling system could work and about aspects to consider. September 2026

This document describes a concept system. Not every aspect would have to be implemented as described here to achieve the desired democratisation. For broader context, you should read and consider endorsing the accompanying Open Letter. You can get involved in the process by emailing us your suggestions or doubts.

We identify two important observations that we think should be addressed in any proposed system that aims to achieve democratisation of the institution.

  1. Existing committee and reporting systems require large time commitments and enable only a limited number of people to work toward change. A collective participatory process would make it possible for everyone to make themselves heard or amplify the voices of others.
  2. A democratized system without any clear, binding commitments does not provide a direct way to hold the institution accountable. Members of staff - especially those who have lost their trust in the institution - are unlikely to engage with even the best system if they think its outcome can be ignored.

Aspects of an Institutional Polling System

In order to allow the broadest possible input, and allow collective participation, we propose something best described as an institutional polling system should be created.

A platform for democratisation

An institutional polling system could be built out as an extension of the presently used stem.tudelft.nl (vote.tudelft.nl). This subdomain is already we reasonably well known, exists in English and Dutch variants and is a sensible subdomain for TU Delft matters related to democratisation. The current platform of stem.tudelft.nl is powered by an external solution provider (progressonderwijs.nl), which may be limited in its extendability. We will not consider these limitations here.

Scope and Visibility

By running the proposed institutional polling system through stem.tudelft.nl, two aspects of the system become immediately clear:
  1. Voting is secure and restricted to TU Delft employees (and students)
  2. Voting is an internal TU Delft process. The broader public and media is not able to access the results unless explicitly included.

Restricting voting to TU Delft staff and students is a logical requirement, as is precisely those that make up the organisation that should decide on its future. Outside input on matters regarding TU Delft decisionmaking should not be handled by this system.

Keeping the voting internal in its initial phases seems appropiate, to avoid creating undue media attention in the general case. This aspect is not set in stone and will tie into the accountability and the communication sections below. In first principle, communication of current state of a an institutional polling or outcome of a close polling should be handled through parties such as Delta, the TU Delft media communication or the poll organisers; rather than a public, live, website link.

Creation process

In line with the spirit of democratisation, it should be possible for any member of the TU Delft to create an institutional poll. In order to prevent the creation of an excessive number of polls, it might be reasonable to require certain conditions for poll creation. Some possible options might be:
  • Requiring a quorum of TU Delft members to create a poll. Requiring a small group of e.g. ~10 people to all become active intiators of a poll would set a certain 'effort' threshold.
  • Restricting student from creating polls, or explicitly limiting the range of topics to those relating to student matters (similar to the scopes of existing medezeggenschap approaches)
Whether the organisers of a poll should have the option to remain anonymous is a difficult matter. Fearing relaliation from management, organisers may abstain from creating a poll on a topic that nonetheless would benefit from broad collective input. At the same time, there should always be a way to contact the organisers of a poll in matters of moderation or other logistical matters. An option for organisers to remain anonymous on the 'public' face of the poll, while their names are visible to platform maintainers might be a compromise. This would be soft-anonymity; trust that the platform maintainers do not share the names under reasonable conditions would be required. An institutional poll should require the following elements
  • A clear title for the stem.tudelft.nl listing
  • A description of the observed probem
  • A decription of the proposed action
  • An explicit description of what a vote is for (the 'vote-action')
These fields shoud be immutable elements; they cannot be changed during the runtime of the poll. This way, a long-running poll can develop, while those that have cast their vote, can still trust that their vote is not 'repurposed' from something after they have cast it.

Some changes to the poll to reflect a developing situation should still be possible. See 'poll lifecycle' below.

At creation time, each poll shall be assigned a unique ID that will also be used in the URL. For, example 'stem.tudelft.nl/poll/dcfe42d4'

Voting Process

The default position should be that any member of the TU Delft (student, researcher, support staff) can vote on a poll. For some applications, restricting input to a subset may be appropriate. Simple filtering could be of type:
  • Restricting only to employees
  • Restricting to specific faculties or university services
Excessively narrow scopes for voting eligiblity should be restricted.

Polls that one is not eligible to vote for should still show up on the stem.tudelft.nl frontpage, but should end up below eligible and restricted polls and should be clearly indicated. This is to maintain a transparent platform and build trust in the platform.

Casting a vote on the same project can only be done once, and a separate page (or page filter) on stem.tudelft.nl should quickly show you the polls you voted in, what you voted, and when you voted. Given the lifecycle of a poll, this page could also show how many updates have occured to the poll since someone voted in the poll. This would encourage participation in the poll and make the process more engaging.

When voting for a project, the default behaviour will be to receive emails about poll updates. The user does not need to enter an email (as their TU mailbox can be used), but should be given the option to opt-out of update emails.

The vote cast by a user is anonymous by default. Ideally, there should be no way for the system administrators to recover this information, but it might be an acceptable compromise to use a system that relies on trust where the system administrators could, in principle, get a list of who voted and in what way, but never share this with any levels of management.

The platform should allow the users to retract their vote at any time as long as the poll is active. When the poll is closed, those that have voted in the poll should receive an email notification, and if the user has placed their vote before the latest poll update (see 'poll lifecycle'), they will still have (e.g.) 7 days to retract or change their vote.

Moderation

If any member, or group of members a the TU Delft can create an institutional poll, then there will be the need for moderation options. Both for polls that explicitly violate the code of conduct or otherwise violate 'good conduct'. (e.g. threats, discrimination). On top of misconduct, duplicate entries, or non-malicious misuse of the platform would also need to be moderated.

The nature of moderation is, in general, a tricky matter. We propose the following aspects to aid in moderation.

  1. Each poll should come with a report button. Reports are by default anonymous and would alert the platform maintainers.
  2. If a poll is deemed unsuitable for the platform, it will receive the status 'restricted' and editing of any of the polls' content is made restricted. Voting is also paused and hidden from the internally visible listing. A change to 'restricted' must be accompanied with an explicit message explaining the motivation for the status change.
  3. Restricted polls are listed at the bottom of the active polls list.
  4. Creator(s) of a restricted poll can still see the (locked) voting tally.
This set of conditions will enable moderation, but should also provide a way in which creators and participants of polls will know that their polls cannot vanish without a trace.

Poll Lifecycle

On top of the immutable poll fields, a poll will have various lifecycle stages. During the active runtime of the projects, organisers should be able to add updates to the project. These updates should be clearly indicated as such, and should be separate from the original, immutable, poll elements. These updates could be calls to actions, invitations to discussion meetings, or relevant updates regarding activity or decisions that have taken place at the TU delft. For an 'active' poll, the poll can change state to the following:
  • Closed/Complete (set by the organisers)
  • Retracted (set by the organisers)
  • Restricted (set by moderators)

In all cases, there is still a visible record of the poll in the system, and URL links to the poll should still resolve to an item. Closed/complete polls should no longer allow active voting on the poll, but should otherwise be identical to active polls. A clear indication of the 'closed/complete' status should be visible.

Organisers of polls have the option to retract polls. This is mostly intended for technical issues or if an old poll is deemed sufficiently misformulated. In most cases, closing an old poll is the preferred option. Retracted polls should only list the title, and (if not anonymous) the organisers.

Accountability

A polling system as described above with no furher action would provide a safer and more direct way to engage with TU Delft matters in a collective way, but it would still be hard to see why one should take the (limited) time out of their day to participate.

Rather than relying on additional work by the poll organisers in trying to find a listening ear, a certain number of votes could automatically trigger actions. This has two effects: (1) The system becomes an actual driver of change, as more participation is guaranteed to result in some action and (2) the system has a clear reason for TU Delft members to participate: if you vote, there is a clear path to action.

Pre-defined triggers based on the number or fraction of eligible voters parttaking in an institutional poll can gradually encourage further participation or drive engagement with the organisation.

We will propose some example triggers below. The exact threshold or nature of the triggers would require fine-tuning. Your input on this matter is welcome. Some possible triggers in order of increasing required engagement:

  1. Auto-greenlighting organisation of a meeting on the topic; to provide a space for in-person discussion. This would avoid the difficulty where individual (e.g.) deans need to decide whether they should permit such events.
  2. An automatic email sent to all TU Delft members highlighting that a poll is reaching a lot of traction and asking for their participation. This both encourages democratisation and participation in that specific poll, but also serves as a mechanism to communicate to TU Delft members that they have ways ('medezeggenschap') in this instituion.
  3. A formal response from the institution or relevant management structure within (e.g.) 60 days regarding the current state or outcome of the poll. In this response, the institution would have to engage with the content of the poll, rather than just acknowledging its existence.

Regardless of which of these triggers could be agreed to by the instition, the existence of an institutional polling system would already provide a 'soft' power to those looking to have 'medezeggenschap'. Even if you still have to ask any level of management for permission for the organisation of a discussion meeting - having (e.g.) 100 verifiable TU Delft votes will help you in your case. Even if no automatic email is sent to all TU Delft employees, you can still directly email your colleagues a safe TU Delft-managed domain where they can securely and anonymously be heard. Even if the institution will not commit itself to respond automatically to a large group of responses; those that organise around the poll topic can show that their cause is important to the members of the TU Delft.

Critically, what this system provides regardless of triggers, is a way to have a collective voice. The institution can use delay tactics or ignore small groups of employees indefinitely - but cannot do so for a large collective call.

Communication

In principle, the institutional polling system is internal for TU Delft employees and students. It may be desirable in some cases to take the discussion outside this scope and include other partners. This may be in cases where a question that is also receiving broad consideration in broader society may be considered. This may involve matters of integrity, who we work with, or what societal matters the TU Delft priortises.

If required, a dislaimer message on the polling page could remind users that the polling is an internal TU Delft matter.

If deemed that communication of the poll results is important with a broader audience outside the TU Delft, this would likely be best left to the media office or Delta.

Members of the TU Delft should feel free to create, engage with and vote in institutional polls. That requires communication in online spaces, but also on campus. Within the TU Delft and TU Delft campus, discussion of institutional polls should always be possible, especially when not of 'restricted' state. That means that promotional material asking for participation in a poll, discussion meetings or bringing up the polls in meetings should -in principle- be desirable. This would reflect a broader commitment to democratisation.
